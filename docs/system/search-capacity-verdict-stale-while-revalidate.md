### A search takes the last capacity verdict and refreshes it behind itself

Round 4 of the search stall (15 Nov 2026). With the earlier rounds in place, 118 probe searches 20 s apart all took 4 to 7 s (no stall, no timeout). The worker's own timing line showed 3.7 s of each search spent in the capacity probe and 0.3 s in the search.

Why it ran again. The probe is an embeddings COUNT plus an unpickle of the whole segment metadata (the pickle holds a dict entry per drawer; on a synthetic two-million-drawer palace the COUNT took 0.25 s and the unpickle 1.3 s). A verdict is reused while the files that feed it are unchanged, but for at most a ceiling, and for a verdict whose outcome can change with the clock the ceiling shrank to 10 s as the metadata pickle aged. That rule was written for palaces without an explicit `hnsw:sync_threshold`, whose standing flush lag turns "diverged" once the pickle is 300 s old; it applied to every palace, so a palace with an explicit threshold, a small standing lag and an old pickle paid the whole probe for every search spaced more than 10 s apart. Whether the live volume instead has coarse timestamps is not known: the first probe per palace now logs which rule applies and the stamp evidence.

Decision. (1) A worker search takes a verdict that is past its ceiling but whose files are unchanged (the stat signature and SQLite change counter are compared on every call) and one background thread per palace probes again; a changed file always forces the probe in the call, and a verdict that is not "ok", one older than an hour, one with coarse timestamps, and one the clock is about to turn are never served stale. (2) The probe no longer unpickles an unchanged pickle: the count is kept by the file's `(inode, mtime_ns, size)` (an hour on fine timestamps, 10 s on coarse), so a write between searches costs the COUNT, not the unpickle. (3) The clock rule applies only to a collection without an explicit sync threshold. The probe never runs under the request lock of another request: it is a file read, a search holds the shared read lock only. The 300 s expiry of a fine-stamp verdict is now a background refresh, not a wait. One busy answer that took 12 s: the worker's own lock wait of 5 s was not charged to the request; the worker now bounds it by what the request has left (`X-Mempalace-Lock-Wait`), and the time the request spent in earlier worker calls is logged per call.
