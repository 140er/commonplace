### Deck link count badge

Decision: under the Deck presentation a device with more than three link ends shows its count, instead of omitting bundles as the first Deck build did.

The Deck map draws every link as its own stroke and has no end marks, so the classic collapsed-end badge (worst state plus count, placed clear of the disc) does not apply. Without any sign, a device with many overlapping strokes looked like a device with fewer links, so users could not tell links were stacked. The shared scene now counts link ends for the presentation too and hands the node a plain count with no state and no position; the Deck card draws it as an end mark on the card edge with the count in a chip beyond it. Selecting a link leaves the count whole because all strokes stay drawn. Three links or fewer stay unmarked, and a map without link targets draws no count. The classic look is unchanged.

Rejected: reusing the classic position search (it clears a disc and label plates the Deck card does not have), and a state-coloured badge (the card already carries the node state).

### Where the mark sits

The mark belongs where the links meet the device, with the count on the outer side. The first Deck build pinned the chip to the top-right corner, so a device whose links came in from the left, or from above, showed its count on the opposite side. The scene now hands each Deck card the paths of its strokes (from the card outwards) and the current zoom. The card is drawn at screen size while the strokes are in map units, so the card measures every stroke on the screen, finds where it leaves the card rectangle, and puts the mark on the edge (left, right, top or bottom) where most strokes leave, at their mean position along that edge. The count chip lies beyond the mark, lined up with it and entirely off the card, so it cannot meet the name, role or status mark; the mark reaches over the edge by its 4px radius only. Because the meeting point moves with the zoom and the map redraws on every view change, the mark follows it at every zoom. When strokes split evenly over two edges, the first of right, left, bottom, top wins. The 11px count keeps its screen size because the card is already scaled by the inverse of the zoom. The classic look and its collapsed end mark are unchanged, and one to three links stay unmarked.
