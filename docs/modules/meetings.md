## meetings

- **Paths:** `commonplace/meetings/`, `tests/test_meetings_foundation.py`, `tests/test_meetings_lifecycle.py`
- **Owns:**

  - The foundation of meeting-join bots (`docs/design/meeting-join-bots.md`, slice 1): the workspace file `meetings.sqlite3` (the whole data model of the design note: owner switches, provider connections, people, meetings, participants, segments, notes, chat journal, outbox, events), sealed provider credentials, the owner's switches and connections, each person's opt-in, the kill switch and the read-only `meeting_status` tool. Slice 2 adds the invite and the lifecycle over the fake adapter; capture, transcription, notes and answers are later slices.

  - `adapter.py` is the provider seam: `Adapter`, `Caps`, the event and frame types, a registry that is empty by default, and `FakeAdapter` (in memory, scripted, records what its `test` saw). `parse_link`, `test`, `join`, `leave`, `find`, `events`, `audio` and `post_chat` are in use (`verify` is declared for the first webhook provider); the fake scripts events and audio (`Wait` holds the event stream until its audio was consumed) and records joins, leaves and posts. `seal.py` seals credentials and meeting links with AES-GCM under a per-workspace key derived from the operator's wrapping key file (`COMMONPLACE_MEETINGS_KEY_FILE`, else the file the per-person grants use) with the workspace, the kind and the row id as associated data. `store.py` is the SQLite store, `routes.py` serves `/api/meetings*`, `tools.py` registers `meeting_status`, `cli.py` adds `commonplace meetings status` and `disable (--workspace ID | --all)`.

- **Promises:**

  - `engine.py` is one meeting's lifecycle (`Run`): claim with a lease, join under the server-built name `Notes bot for <inviter>` (two tries), lobby (at most ten minutes, never bypassed), the fixed announcement through the `meeting_outbox`, then the live loop, and the one exit `_conclude`. The capture gate (`Capture`) is closed until the announcement outbox row is `sent` (adapter-confirmed) and audio is not even requested before; it is closed again before anything is discarded, under the lock a frame takes. `Sink` is the seam where frames go (this slice drops them; the capture slice replaces it). `worker.py` is the beat: it recovers meetings whose lease lapsed and starts due ones, and exists only while a meeting is requested or running (an invite starts it, `resume` restarts it after a restart for workspaces that already have unfinished meetings). `store.py` also holds the invite (sealed link, per-day counter table `meeting_days` inside `meetings.sqlite3`), compare-and-set transitions, the lease and the outbox; `routes.py` adds `POST /api/meetings`, `GET /api/meetings/<id>`, `POST .../leave` (inviter) and `POST .../stop` (owner).

  - Off until configured at every level: with the master switch off (the default, and the state of a workspace that never used meetings) every `/api/meetings*` route except the owner's own settings answers a member 404 and the owner 409 `meetings_disabled`, `meeting_status` is hidden, and reading creates no file. A person who did not opt in cannot invite (403 `not_opted_in`); an invite also needs an enabled connection. An invite also needs the attestation tap, a link the provider's `parse_link` accepts (the link is sealed, never echoed or logged, wiped when the meeting ends), a provider that can send chat, and room under `daily_meetings`.

  - Credentials and meeting links are write-only: sealed before they are stored, never returned by any route or tool, never in a log line, audit row or error text (adapter error text is replaced by a fixed reason code). If the key file is missing, unreadable, not the service user's or inside the data directory, the write is refused (503 `key_store_unavailable`) and nothing is stored. A new connection is off; it can only be switched on after a passing test (`not_tested` 409); new credentials or a failing test switch it off again; a provider without chat send cannot pass.

  - The owner sees settings, connections without credentials and counts per state, never titles or text; a member sees only whether meetings are on and the limits, and only their own opt-in. Another person, agent token or workspace is 404. `meeting_status` returns the caller's own meetings with state, times and counts, no text.

  - Consent is fail-closed: nothing is captured until the announcement is delivered. If chat send is missing, the post fails twice or its outcome is unknown, the bot leaves and the meeting ends `failed:no_announcement` with nothing stored. A host removing the bot (`removed`), anyone sending exactly `bot stop` (a message that merely contains it, such as the bot's own announcement echoed back, does not count), the owner's stop, a kill switch, the person or their membership or the connection going away at a beat, or a worker error all close the gate, discard what was captured (segments, participants, queued posts), leave and end the meeting (`discarded`, `cancelled` or `failed:internal`); the owner's stop and `bot stop` post one fixed notice first. `max_minutes`, the inviter's leave and the meeting's end keep what was captured and go `ending` -> `processing` -> `done` or `timeout`. A crashed or restarted worker leaves its meeting to expire: the bot leaves, posts in flight become `unknown` and it ends `interrupted`, never rejoined. Late joiners get a reminder at most every ten minutes. A repeated `request_id` is the same meeting, a second fire of a scheduled join does nothing and `max_concurrent` holds extra meetings in `requested`.

  - The kill switch (`PUT enabled:false`, or `commonplace meetings disable`) ends every active meeting as cancelled, wipes its join link, deletes its working segments and cancels its queued chat posts; generated notes stay. A workspace without a `meetings.sqlite3` is left untouched.

- **How it fails:**

  - Refusals carry a code: `invalid_field`, `invalid_value`, `invalid_limit`, `invalid_label`, `invalid_secrets`, `invalid_language`, `invalid_wing`, `unknown_provider` (400); `not_opted_in` (403); `meetings_disabled`, `not_tested`, `label_in_use`, `in_use`, `no_connection`, `transcript_not_allowed` (409); `key_store_unavailable` (503); `attestation_required`, `invalid_link`, `invalid_title`, `invalid_board`, `invalid_disclosure`, `invalid_request_id`, `invalid_start`, `unknown_connection`, `connection_required` (400); `no_chat_send` (409); `daily_limit` (429).

  - Not built yet (later slices): transcription and capture, notes and filing, deleting a meeting, in-meeting answers, the provider-event intake route and the first real provider adapter (so a real hub has no provider yet and an invite waits for one). The inviter is not yet told who typed `bot stop` (the meeting journal carries no text).

- **Tests:**

  - `tests/test_meetings_lifecycle.py` covers off-by-default (no worker, no join, no file), invite validation and idempotency, the announcement-first and nothing-before-`sent` rule (fake call order), failed, errored and unknown announcements, lobby, removal, `bot stop`, owner stop, leave, `max_minutes`, reminders, the kill switch and standing checks, crash recovery and fail-closed errors, scheduled joins, concurrency, and the access, leak and no-audio-on-disk checks. `tests/test_meetings_foundation.py` covers the clean-workspace case (every route, every role, no file, no tool), the owner switch and its bounds, the test-before-enable rule, sealing (binding, tamper, missing or misplaced key), a sentinel-token leak check across responses, logs, audit rows and the database file, owner-only routes, person opt-in and validation, the kill switch over HTTP and CLI, `meeting_status` and the fake adapter.
