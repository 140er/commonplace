- **Owns:** How the capacity verdict (`hnsw_capacity_status`) is reused for a search. A worker search runs inside `serve_stale_capacity_verdicts()`: when the cached verdict is past its reuse ceiling but its files are unchanged (the stat signature and SQLite's change counter are compared on every call), it is served at once and one background thread per palace probes again (`capacity-refresh`, `wait_for_capacity_refresh()` joins it). A verdict that is not "ok", is older than an hour, has coarse timestamps, or is one the clock is about to turn is never served stale, and a changed file always forces the probe in the call. The reuse ceiling of a collection that stores an explicit `hnsw:sync_threshold` no longer shortens as the metadata pickle ages (only a collection without one has a verdict that turns diverged with time). The probe skips the unpickle of `index_metadata.pickle` while the file's `(inode, mtime_ns, size)` is unchanged (one hour on fine timestamps, 10 s on coarse). The first probe per palace logs one line, `capacity verdict reuse: fine timestamps=… ceiling …s, clock-dependent=…, explicit sync threshold=…, probe took …s`, with the evidence; a stale answer logs `capacity verdict served stale`, a background probe of a second or more logs its duration.
- **Promises:** A search after a pause between searches does not wait for the O(palace) probe (it cost 3.7 s on the live palace, 1.3 s of it the unpickle on a synthetic two-million-drawer one); the verdict it uses was valid for the unchanged files and is refreshed behind it. A write between searches still makes the probe run, but without the unpickle while the pickle is unchanged. Callers outside a worker search keep the synchronous rule.
- **How it fails:** A coarse-timestamp filesystem keeps the 10 s ceiling and probes in the call; a probe that fails in the background leaves the old verdict to be probed again by the next search that finds it past its limit.
