- **Owns:**

  - `lifecycle.py` is the meeting lifecycle as data: the states, the events that may change them (claim, advance, leave, stop, finish keeping or discarding, recover, kill) and a table of which events are legal in which state and what each may write. `MeetingStore._transition` is the only code that writes `meetings.state`: the claim, the worker's steps, the inviter's leave, the owner's stop, the kill switch, a normal end and crash recovery all go through it, and it applies what the table says with a compare-and-set on the state it just read, inside one write transaction.

- **Promises:**

  - The rules live in the table, not in each writer. An ended state never changes. A stop (`removed`) is left only by a discarding end: whatever ends a stopped meeting (a removal, a failure, the kill switch, recovery) ends it `discarded` with its segments and participants deleted, and a keeping end (`done`, `timeout`) is refused for it. `ending` and `processing` never go back to joining, announcing or live. A kept end is only legal from `processing`, which is only reached through `ending`. A stop or a leave that lands in any non-ended state is recorded and cannot be overwritten by a step that was already in flight: that step's compare-and-set is refused and the worker acts on the request at its next look. A stop in `requested` cancels it. Participants are only written while a meeting is `live`, so a late event cannot write back what an end deleted.

  - Capture opens only from the table: `live` is entered from `announcing` only, and only with the adapter-confirmed announcement stamped; the capture gate opens only after that step succeeded and re-reads the state under the lock a frame takes, so a stop or leave that landed first keeps it closed. A stop or leave during the announcement post therefore ends the meeting with the gate never opened (a stop discards and posts its notice because the announcement was already sent; a leave keeps nothing since nothing was captured), and a stop that lands at any step of a normal end (`ending`, `processing` or just before the final write) still ends it `discarded`.

  - Every end has an owner and nothing stays active once its worker is idle. The worker's ends go through `Run._conclude`, which also ends, as a failure, a meeting it can no longer drive; an orphan (its lease lapsed) is ended by `engine.recover`, from any running state: `interrupted`, keeping what was already stored (the design note's recovery; the audio buffer died with the worker), or `discarded` if a stop had been requested. The first end reason recorded wins, except that a stop's and a discarding end's replace it. A meeting is never rejoined.

  - The lease is renewed by a small heartbeat while a provider call blocks, stopping a little after the hard `max_minutes` limit, so a slow join or post is not ended `interrupted` while its worker runs. The worker beat only starts as many meetings as `max_concurrent` allows, and decides to go idle under the lock `kick` takes, so an invite never waits for a restart.

- **Tests:**

  - `tests/test_meetings_transitions.py` checks the table as data (closed, every state reachable and able to end, every event and target answered by the rules), that `UPDATE meetings` appears only in `_transition` and the lease renewal, every active state against every event in every order with the step a worker has in flight, and the worker over the fake provider with a stop, leave, kill, recovery, clock jump or crash before each store call it makes (journal is a path of the table, the meeting ends and the beat goes idle, a stopped meeting ends discarded, capture never opens after a stop).
